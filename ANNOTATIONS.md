# Concorde
affectionally dubbed ***l'Oiseau Blanc***
## Ogival Delta Wing
**Main Features**
- 6 elevons controlling rolling and pitch instead of the conventional elevators on the tail and ailerons on the wings
- Slenderness allows it to produce sufficient lift at a wide range of AoA and even above angles which other wings might stall at. This allows it to fly at a range of speeds, simply by changing its AoA, much like a bird. 
- At high speeds, Concorde flew with a low AoA to minimise drag. At low speeds, AoA is large. 
- It is forced to fly at high AoA, during slow-speeds takeoff and landing due to the absence of flaps or slats
- swept-back, triangular shape kept the wing edges behind the shock wave generated by the nose, reducing wave drag at supersonic speeds. the result of that is reduced lift-generation due to poor L/D ratio characteristic of delta wing. to compensate, the concorde had to fly at high AoA at low speeds to be able to generate sufficient lift, to be able to harness vortex lift (more on that below). and to fly at high AoA, it needed a drooping nose for the pilot to see. 

- so why ogival?
***a complex mathematical shape derived for minimising drag at supersonic speeds***

inner part has a very high sweepback
outer part has a lower sweepback, help creates vortex lift (from the leading edge of wings)

specifically listened to maximise vortex lift and stability at low speeds (of takeoff and landing)
and less drag at high speeds. 

- so why delta? 
delta excel in supersonic regimes
large wing area, reduce wing loading, improve maneuvarability. 
strong strucutrally, large volume for internal fuel.

**VORTEX LIFT** 
Comparing between commercial aircrafts with very mildly swept wings and Concorde's delta swept wings
Takeoff and landings require slow speeds!! 
Technically to prevent stalling, you fly at a higher speed so airspeed increasing increases the dynamic pressure or energy hitting your wing. 
But takeoff and landing needs slow speed for SAFETY and infrastructure. to minimise take-off distance and landing distance. brakes must dissipate a huge amount of energy. 

During slow speeds of takeoff and landing,
- On conventional wing (high aspect ratio): if you push the angle of attack slightly higher, the airflow will separate entirely. The suction pressure will collapse resulting in a deep stall. Normal aircrafts can use flaps or slats to change the shape of wing, allowing it to fly slwoly while keeping duselage flat and level.
- On delta wings, separation is not an issue at high AoA. in fact, it is forced to fly at high AoA. because delta wings have no effective lift at low AOAs due to the short and wide span of the wing. unlike conventional wings with good L/D ratio. 
    - Sharp leading edges designed to intentionally shed vortices. unlike commerical airlines that keep their leading edges ROUNDDD to keep the airflow as attached as long as possible 
    - At high AoA, vortices are shed, that envelop the entire upper surface of the wing, increasing the suction and lfit. VORTEX LIFT. that air separation is CONTROLLED and organised, does not cause turbulent stall unlike conventional wings. 



Wingtip vortices create lift that is fundamental to Concorde's ability to fly slowly during take off and landing

Due to the absence of flaps or slats like conventional aircrafts, they rely on vortex lift primarly. Vortex lift only comes into play at high AoA, where lift coefficiento of delta wings massively increases.
